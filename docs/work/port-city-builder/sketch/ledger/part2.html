Your spec

  1. Vehicles drive themselves and come in any size, from one seat to a bus.
  2. Nobody drives inside the city, so nothing is parked there.
  3. Daily needs sit within a walk: leisure, a library, non-ER medical and fresh food.
  4. Parks with tennis, pickleball and basketball courts are nearby. Larger fields are a slightly longer walk or a short ride.
  5. Nothing moves near pedestrians that can cause harm.
  6. No one needs a vehicle near their door. People walk to the movers, which run on a sub level.
  7. Freight reaches the door with a porter and a powered cart.
  8. Bikes have segregated lanes.
  9. Groups of houses share a single sub-level entry point.
  10. Housing is townhouses, with no stand-alone homes. The middle is mixed-use: government, an auditorium, offices and condos.
  11. Height graduates from the outer edges to the middle.
  12. Every residence and office has a dumbwaiter for deliveries, so the market can shrink.
  13. There are still farmers markets or produce stores, butchers and bakeries.
  14. There are cafés, casual dining and fine dining.
  15. Elementary schools hold at most 500 pupils (K–5), middle schools 1,000 (6–8) and high schools 1,500 (9–12).
  16. Kids and adults can walk up to 30 minutes to reach school or work.
  17. All other walks are uncapped, because there are people movers.
  18. The city has to stretch: land can grow, housing can grow or shrink, and density and school population move.
  19. The middle is a circle of 25-story buildings.

What I filled in

  • The stretch band is 20% either way. You named what stretches and not how far.
  • People switch from walking to a mover at a 15-minute walk. Line 17 sets no cap, and the ledger still needs the switch.
  • Townhouses are three stories.
  • A ring building has 2,000 m² floors, and the buildings stand a building's width apart.
  • The elementary school is shared between centers, now that line 16 lets it be.
  • There is one mover stop under each center and one under the ring. The entries lead to a walkway, because you said people still walk to the movers. Step 4 has the dial that brings the stops closer.
  • A group that shares an entry is everyone within a one-minute walk of it.
  • Bike lanes run along the edges between neighborhoods.
  • The goods pipe carries staples, parcels and trash.
  • Fire crews and ambulances arrive below and work from inside the buildings. That follows from line 5 and is not costed.
  • Every field in the ledger marked assumed.

What a need costs in walking

The farthest home from one of anything, by how many people share it and how tall the townhouses are. A café needs 500 people, a doctor's office about 4,000 and a full 1,500-pupil high school about 31,000.

The same premises at other sizes

Both columns at four populations. Past the ceiling in step 2 the rim is farther from the one middle than line 16 allows, so the larger sizes show what gives. Trips that go to no center, shared place or middle go to a random spot in the city, so the big-city distances run long.

Not in the ledger yet

  • Trips that leave the city, and the edge where the movers meet the outside world.
  • What any of it costs: the sub level, the goods pipe and the ring.
  • A second middle, for a city past the ceiling in step 2.
  • How fire and ambulance crews work with no vehicles on the surface.
  • People who cannot manage the walk to a stop. Line 6 leaves them a chair or a cart slower than the harm line, and step 4 counts their trips.
  • Chauffeuring. About 8% of US car trips are a driver ferrying someone else. They should mostly vanish here, and the ledger keeps them.
  • Who lives here. The ledger uses the US age mix, and a new city of townhouses would likely have more children.
  • Where the jobs are, beyond the offices.
  • The rest of a neighborhood: childcare, worship, a gym, a barber. Their floor area is counted. Their trips are treated as going anywhere in the city.
  • People choosing nearer places over farther ones.
  • Industry, institutions, airports and vacant land, in either column.
  • Buses and trains in the car column. They carry 2.5% of US trips.
