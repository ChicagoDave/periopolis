# Run

**Purpose.** Twenty years on the clock. Crews turn orders into buildings over years, people move in as their homes are finished (or, on a run with growth, month by month at the citywide pace, ADR-0009), and the stretches line 18 allows for happen to the city on a timeline dealt at the start of a run. Each year the city is checked as it stands, and every broken line counts one line-year against the run.

**Package.** `@city/run` (`domain/run`). Pure and deterministic; depends on `@city/site` alone. The one random source is a seeded generator, so a run replays from its seed. Public interface: `startRun`, `stopRun`, `tick`, `tickMonth`, the month clock (`growthOn`, `monthStep`, `occupiedOf`, `homesOf`), who may live where (`admit`, `limitsOf`, `childlessOf`), the crew order (`classOf`, `crewed`, `emergencyOf`), `placeOrder`, `cancelJob`, `putFirst`, `inHand`, the run ordering for itself (`inferStep`, `homesAhead`, `cancelOrder`; ADR-0015), `notices`, `stressAt`, `restoreClock`, `totalSpend`, and the rules.

**Consumers.** The Board shows the clock card, the work in hand and the notices, and turns clicks into orders. Since 2026-10-19 every run the Board starts is on the month clock unless the player asks for the yearly mode, and the Board's own timer calls `tickMonth` once a month at a chosen speed; the Run's default (`growth` absent, the sketch's clock) and the parity suite are unchanged.

## The year

1. The first ten jobs with work left each lose a year.
2. Finished jobs open on the layout, neighborhoods before what stands in them. A school whose neighborhood is not there yet, or a module whose site is not, stays ready.
3. A closure announced this year picks its stretch: one of the closable stretches, from the seed and the event's id. With no line there is nothing to close and no notice.
4. The city is checked under this year's stress. The row goes in the record and its broken lines into the line-years.
5. What stands is priced under the same stress; the row carries the standing by layer and the year's spend against the row before.

The squeeze is over before the wave arrives, so no two events push the same line past the band. That is what makes the promise hold.

## The promise

Each event is one of the four stretch tests come true. A city where every line holds and all four stretch tests hold cannot be broken by anything the clock deals. The ledger's city with twelve more stretches, so every stop has a second way, is left alone for twenty years on 25 timelines and loses 0 line-years. On spokes it loses 3 or 4, all in closure years.

## Scenarios

- **Mechanics**: a reactor's first module takes three years with its site and the second one year, and one order adding two modules to a new reactor brings one site, not two (fixed 2026-10-20 when the run first ordered two at once); a school ordered on a building site waits for its homes and opens with them; twelve one-year jobs on ten crews leave two waiting until one is put first; ordering a finished stretch again takes it down and ordering one in hand cancels it; canceling a reactor site drops its later module instead; canceling a neighborhood drops its schools; stopping the clock finishes the work in hand at once.
- **Founding 100,000 from nothing**: everything else ordered ahead of the homes, 151 crew-years in all. Left to run, 3 line-years; with a bypass laid when each closure is announced, 0 or 1. Homes for 100,000 by year 20 on every seed tried.
- **What a founding spends**: from an empty site, the spend summed over twenty years is the cost of what stands at year 20 plus the running bills of years 1 to 20; summed over layers it equals summed over years. A stretch finishes in a year and is paid before the neighborhood's floor; no mover is bought until someone rides; a stretch taken down comes off that year's groundworks. Seed 42, a ring, a reactor and four neighborhoods on spokes ordered at year 0: 4.9 billion to build and 4.1 to run over twenty years, 9.1 in all.

- **The sized city founded from nothing, with growth** (seed 42): every piece ordered at year 0. Neighborhoods open from year 2; from year 3 people arrive at the full 4,800 a year, Irvine's pace, and the city grows by 2,600 to 4,700 a year net of leavers; it is full, 50,000, in year 16, after which 2,175 a year leave and as many arrive. While a neighborhood fills it is too empty to keep its daily list; that is a watch, not a broken line, so filling counts no line-years (David, 2026-10-14). By year 20 no line is broken. With the homes full, twelve months of running spend equal the year's to the cent.
- **Built in order, people when served** (ADR-0012, `inOrder`; from `docs/work/tests/city-261015.txt`). Given an empty site and, ordered at year 0 in this order, six neighborhoods round a ring building with three elementary schools and two sets of fields, then the mover line through all six, a reactor, and a water, a sewage and a waste unit:
  - When the clock runs, then the stretches, the ring, the reactor and the plant units have crews before any neighborhood, and the neighborhoods are worked last, in the order they were placed.
  - When a neighborhood is finished but its water, sewage, waste or power is not open for its people, then it holds nobody, the clock says what it lacks, and its own breaks are a watch that counts no line-years.
  - When the one reactor module carries the peak only while it runs, then people move in all the same, lines 20 and 21 are broken and count, and the clock says it takes 2 modules (amended 2026-10-18; before that the founding as placed stood empty for twenty years).
  - When the plants and the reactor carry it but it has no stop on a finished line to the ring, then everyone may move in; line 6 and line 17 are broken and count; while its stretches are in hand, line 6 says the line to it is under construction.
  - When the plants and the reactor carry it but its pupils have no seats within a 30-minute walk, then only the childless share moves in, with no pupils, and the clock says it lacks school seats; when its seats open, it fills.
  - When a lived-in neighborhood is left short of water, sewage, waste or power by a squeeze, nobody leaves, the line is broken and counts, and a unit of the short kind in hand takes a crew ahead of everything else.
  - Without `inOrder` the same orders play exactly as the sketch's clock: homes first, 30,000 people at year 2, line 6 broken (the parity suite).
- **The ledger's city with growth**: full from the start, it refills its 181 leavers a month and stays at 50,000; it records the same lines and the same capital spend as the run without growth.

- **The city grows itself** (ADR-0015, `infer`; `docs/work/archive/sim-infers-growth/plan.md`):
  - Given one neighborhood on an empty site and a run with growth, in order and `infer`, when the first month passes, then the ring, two modules, the three plant units, the three schools, the fields and the stretches to the ring are in the work in hand as inferred orders, each recorded on the month with the need it answers, and the player has placed nothing.
  - Given the same run, when its homes ahead of arrivals fall below 12,000, then one neighborhood is ordered that month on the open tile nearest the ring, recorded with `homes` as its reason, and never more than one a month; from an empty site the first month orders the first neighborhood.
  - Given the same run left alone for twenty years, then the people grow at the pace and the spend summed over layers equals the cost of what stands plus the running bills, as any run's does. On the real path (seed 42): 56,349 people in 14 neighborhoods, every line held at year 20, 5 line-years (the ring the lone neighborhood lacks in its first year, and three times a piece open a year before its stretch); from nothing, 53,698 in 13, 3 line-years. David's founding of 2026-10-15 grows to 48,379 people in 12 neighborhoods with every line held and 0 line-years, where by hand it ends at 18,000 held for seats with 46; its first residents come in year 7 rather than 5, the crews building what the run ordered first.
  - Given a run with `infer` and the same pieces ordered by hand on a run without it, then both pay the same sum to build what stands at year 20.
  - Given a need the player has already ordered, when the month passes, then the run orders nothing for it.
  - Given an inferred job the player cancels, when the need re-arises, then the run does not order it again; when the player orders that piece themselves, it is theirs and the veto is lifted. Given a player's own piece taken down, then the run orders what the need asks the next month.
  - Given people living there left short of water by a squeeze, when the month passes, then a water unit is ordered that month and takes a crew ahead of every class.
  - Given a need the board has no room for, then it is recorded on the month as `NoRoom` and nothing is ordered for it.
  - Given `infer` asked for without `growth` or without `inOrder`, then `startRun` refuses with an error. Given a run without `infer`, then the clock, the record and the parity fixtures are byte-identical to today's.
  - Given a stored run with `infer` and its vetoes, when it is read back, then it carries on inferring and the vetoes hold; stored without vetoes, it restores with none.
  - Given a piece the run built that the player takes down, or a job the run placed that the player's order cancels, then it is vetoed and the run does not build it there again; given the player's own piece taken down, then nothing is remembered. Given the player's order onto a tile the run holds, then the Site's refusal is the answer and the game is unchanged. Given a lived-in city left short of water, then the run orders a water unit that month and, from the year's end, it is crewed ahead of every class. Given the run's orders replayed by hand on a run without `infer`, month for month, then both build the same city and spend the same money to the cent.
  - On the Board: Play on a one-neighborhood city grows it in view with no order from the player; an inferred order does not pause the months and a player's order still does; one Play is one undo step and takes the run's orders back with it; the work in hand marks inferred jobs and Cancel on one is a veto; "Let the city grow itself" is on by default and off keeps today's clock.
- **The city grows past its circle** (ADR-0016 Decision 3; `docs/work/concentric-site/plan.md`, Phase 6):
  - Given a run with `infer` whose first circle holds 36 neighborhoods standing or in hand, when the homes ahead of arrivals fall below the lead and `proposeNeighborhood` finds no open tile in the circle, then that month the run orders `openCircle` at `8,0` through `placeOrder`, the month's row carries `CircleOpened`, and the next month's homes order goes in the second circle; the second circle's plants, ring and line are ordered by the needs signal before its homes are lived in (ADR-0012 Decision 2).
  - Given the same circle full on a run without `infer`, then no circle is opened and `NoRoom` is not recorded, since no order is attempted.
  - Given the player cancels the inferred `openCircle` order, then the circle is vetoed by its middle and the run never opens it again; given the player's own `openCircle`, the veto is lifted and it is theirs.
  - Given a run from an empty circle left to grow itself for forty years (seed 42), then the first circle fills, the second opens the month the first has no room, people live in the second circle's first neighborhood only once its own band serves it, and the spend summed over layers equals the cost of what stands plus the running bills; at twenty years the people are compared against ADR-0015's record (48,379 from the founding, 56,349 from one neighborhood, 53,698 from an empty site) and the difference is written into ADR-0016.

## Acceptance

`domain/run/test/parity.test.ts` matches 64 recorded steps (start, order, tick, first, cancel, stop, with the work in hand after each), the wave table, 7 timelines, 6 restores, the 25 promise runs, and the ledger's city left alone on 2 seeds with every year's notices and stress, against the sketch's `clock.js`. `scenarios.test.ts` asserts the scenarios above and replays the sketch's founding script on 4 seeds against the recorded records. `spend.test.ts` asserts the spend scenarios: year 0, the twenty-year identities, the order of paying, the take-down, and a stored clock reading its money back (a row written before money restores zeros). The sketch's clock has no money, so parity compares the record's old fields only, as ADR-0007 Decision 7 has the ledger do. The sketch's sentences (what an order said) are kept in the fixture for the Board's wording tests in Phase 4. `order.test.ts` asserts the build order (ADR-0012 Decision 2) on David's founding of 2026-10-15: the first year's crews go to the ring, the schools and the first six stretches, not the homes; every stretch and plant stands before the first neighborhood opens; without `inOrder` the same orders open the homes at year 2 with one stretch built; by year 20 the same city stands either way, and each run paid to build exactly what stands (what stands is priced for the people living there, so the two runs pay differently); an order's `startsNow`, the work in hand and putting a home first all follow the classes; a stored run keeps `inOrder` only when it was stored as `true`. `movein.test.ts` asserts who moves in (ADR-0012 Decisions 3 to 9) on the same founding: as placed, with one reactor module, the childless share, 18,000, moves in once the plants and the module open (year 5), held back for middle and high seats, and lines 20 and 21 are broken from the first resident since the module carries the peak only while it runs (Decision 3 as amended 2026-10-18); with a second module the same people move in and lines 20 and 21 hold, with lines 6 and 15 not counted against them; with the schools too nobody until the line, both modules and the plants stand, then all 30,000; with growth they arrive only after that, never faster than 400 a month, and fill; without `inOrder` the homes fill at year 2 and nothing is held back.
