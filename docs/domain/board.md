# Board

**Purpose.** The web adapter: the Future City Builder page. It draws the site, turns clicks into layout commands or crews' orders, words every event, check, issue and notice, and shows the score, the line checks, the stretch tests and the clock. Zoomed into a tile, it draws that tile's ground from the Ground's plan, one level at a time. It holds no rules.

**Package.** `apps/board` (`@city/board`, Vite). Depends on `@city/site`, `@city/run` and `@city/ground` (and `@city/ledger` through them). `pnpm --filter @city/board build` emits one self-contained page at `apps/board/dist/index.html`, so it can still be published the way the sketch was.

## Zooming in

"Look closer" in the inspector, on a neighborhood or a ring, shows that tile's ground in place of the map; the tile map is the zoomed-out view and does not change. The view bar names the tile, switches between the surface and the sub level, and goes back. The view lives in the session only: a reload opens on the map, and a tile that loses its ground (erased, undone, the site cleared) sends the view back to the map on the next redraw. The plan is Ground's; the Board picks a level with `layersAt` and words the caption, the legend and the placeholder note.

## Opening cities

"Load the ledger's city" puts the sketch's 50,000 on the board; "Load it sized to its people" puts the same city with every ring building and reactor module it does not need taken away (`sizedLayout`, ADR-0008): 12.2 billion to build against 13.4. Either stops the clock, and Undo brings the old city back.

## What the city needs

The card under the inspector lists what the layout lacks to keep its lines whole, in the order the Site proposes it, each with what it adds to the bill (a school or fields nearer home can cost less, since fewer people ride). Show marks the tiles a proposal touches; Add builds it, with any plant or ring proposed earlier that its line runs to; Add all builds every one. A whole city reads "The city needs nothing more to keep its lines whole." While the clock runs nothing is proposed, since an order is not built yet and proposing again would order it twice; stop the clock to see what the city needs. A full board says it has no room. A need only the squeeze asks for, in a full circle, says so: "The circle is full, so it is built with room for the squeeze.", and a school's pupils are those "with no seat in a squeeze" (ADR-0018). A school the squeeze asks for in a growing circle says instead "It is built with room for the squeeze and the wave of families.", never that the circle is full (ADR-0020). The size line is never on the list: it waits on people (ADR-0006, 14 of 15).

## Money

"What it costs" (lines 24 to 29, ADR-0007) sits under the stretch tests. A headline gives what stands costs to build and to run, a person and in all, with the Land and Financing lines. A table gives the six layers, the two totals, Land and Financing for this city, a person, and the car city of the same size a person (from the Score's car comparison, which carries the car column's cost). A disclosure lists every row: how many at which unit cost, the cost, and the premise's mark as a chip, **Yours**, **Sourced** with its year (the source in the chip's title), **Sourced, accepted** when David has accepted it, or **Mine**. While the clock runs the card adds what the run has spent so far, by layer and in all, and the record gains a Spent column, one year's outlay. An empty site says nothing costs anything yet; year 0 says nothing is spent yet. A row the Ledger or the Site adds without a name here, or a premise without a unit, fails the type check. The notes under the game say how spend is paid and that every premise has been accepted (2026-10-14), with water following the green ground.

## Built in order

Every run the page starts, yearly or month by month, is built in order (ADR-0012 Decision 1): the line, the ring, the plants and the schools take the crews before fields and homes, and a finished neighborhood is lived in only when the city carries it. Between steps the board is checked for the people the clock admitted, so a neighborhood standing empty reads as empty. The year and the month say who is held back, grouped by what they wait for ("2 neighborhoods stand empty, waiting for power."; "1 neighborhood holds only households with no school-age children, waiting for middle-school and high-school seats."), the inspector says it for the neighborhood picked, and the export lists it as "Held back now". Line 6 says "The mover line to them is under construction." for the neighborhoods the work in hand gives a stop, "Draw it through the other one." for any it does not, and keeps "Draw the mover line through them." when nothing in hand reaches them. A run saved before this decision restores without it and plays the sketch's way.

## The month clock and the day

A run is on the month clock unless "Year by year, as the sketch runs it" is checked, which leaves the sketch's clock as it was: homes fill the year they are finished; flows 3 and 4 check it to keep their pinned figures. On the month clock the city grows itself unless "Let the city grow itself" is unchecked (ADR-0015 Decision 9); flows 10 to 14 uncheck it to keep theirs, and flow 15 holds it: from nothing, the first month orders a neighborhood and the second its ring, plants, schools and line with nothing placed by hand, Cancel on the city's water unit vetoes it, Play founds the city without pausing while the player's own order still pauses, two undos return to where Play was pressed, and a reload keeps the orders the months recorded. On the month clock, "Start the clock on this city" and "Start a city from nothing" put the clock at year 0 with the orders open and the months still; Play lets them run (David, 2026-10-19: the sim runs month by month on its own, with visible progress). A run founded from nothing stands empty until its first neighborhoods open and the city carries them, then fills off the waiting list as far as the homes it may hold allow (ADR-0017; 2,500 join the list a month): two neighborhoods ordered at year 0 with two reactor modules and a unit of each plant open in year 2 and stand empty, waiting for power, while 60,000 wait for a home; the modules open at year 3 and the childless share of both, 6,000, moves in at once, since no school stands; the first month of year 4 keeps them at 6,000 ("21 moved in, 5.3 born, 4.6 died and 21.8 moved away. 86,479 people wait for a home."), held for school seats. The month line and the people-now sentence say how many wait once anyone does, and the month table carries a Waiting column. A month or a year passing says the month; the month clock survives a reload.

The day is a summer weekday (David, 2026-10-14). The ledger's city and the sized city, every neighborhood full: 50,000 people, 147,727 trips, 14,773 of them from 5 to 6 p.m.; power peaks at 75 MW and draws 1,440 MWh; at the busiest hour 188 of the sized city's 217 movers are moving; 7,800 pupils go to school between 7 and 8 a.m. and home between 2 and 3 p.m. An empty site's day has no trips, no power and every mover parked. A population or day premise the Ledger adds without wording here fails the type check.
