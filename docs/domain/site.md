# Site

**Purpose.** The ground the city is laid out on, divided into tiles; the commands that lay it out; and the sixteen line checks and four stretch tests that tell whether the city as drawn still honors the lines.

**Package.** `@city/site` (`domain/site`). Pure and deterministic; depends on `@city/ledger` alone. Public interface: the layout and its commands, `assess`, the rates, the board geometry (the outline as an extent, `ringDistance`, `eastWest`) and the circles (`hexRing`, `circleTiles`, `bandOf`, `circleOf`, `inBand`, `circleFits`, `shoreTiles`; ADR-0016), seating, the line network, and `costOf`, the cost of what stands (with the Ledger's cost names re-exported so the Run depends on the Site alone).

**Consumers.** Run assesses a layout under each year's stress and counts broken lines; the Board draws the layout, turns clicks into commands, and words the findings.

## Terms

| Term | Meaning | Whose word |
|---|---|---|
| Site | The ground the city is laid out on: a rectangle of hexagonal tiles inside an outline, five tile steps across and five rows down from the middle, 115 tiles (ADR-0006). The sketch's board was a hexagon of 91 tiles, which the rectangle holds. *Decided 2026-10-20 (ADR-0016; the board and the circles built in `docs/work/archive/concentric-site/plan.md` Phase 2, the band rule and the second circle in later phases): the rectangle is laid along a lake shore, its south edge, 158 tiles from row -4 to 4 and east-west positions -4 to 13, and holds two circles side by side; ground outside every circle is open to nothing.* | Mine |
| Circle | A central ring tile with three hex rings of neighborhoods round it, every one within the 30-minute walk of the ring, and a fourth hex ring, the band. 61 tiles; up to 36 neighborhoods, 180,000 people. A layout carries its circles' middles; the first is at `0,0`, the second at `9,0`, so their bands touch at a seam and no tile is in two (8 steps would share the tile between). `openCircle` lays out another: ground, not a piece, open the moment it answers (ADR-0016). | Yours (2026-10-20) |
| Band | The fourth hex ring of a circle, 24 tiles, where the plants stand and nothing else does. A plant off a band is refused `OutsideBand`; a neighborhood or ring building on one, `InsideBand`; anything on a tile outside every circle, `OutsideCircle`. The band's south tiles touch the shore; the water and sewage plants and the reactors stand on that side, intake upstream of outfall (ADR-0016). | Yours (2026-10-20) |
| Shore | The south edge of the site; the lake beyond it is off the board (line 30). The current runs along it in a signed direction, a premise of the site (`SiteRates.current`, Mine). Upstream and downstream are by a tile's east-west position, `q + r/2`. | Yours (2026-10-20) |
| Outline | The site's shape: which tiles are on it. A hexagon of a given radius, or a rectangle of a given half width and half height, both centred on `0,0`. | Mine |
| Tile | One hexagon of the site, named `q,r`: the catchment of one center. 5,000 people on 93.3 acres; a 9.9-minute walk from one middle to the next. | Mine |
| Layout | What stands on each tile and where the mover line runs. A value: commands make new ones and never change the one they were given. | Mine |
| Neighborhood | A tile of 5,000 people with its daily list built in. It may hold an elementary, middle or high school, and playing fields. | Mine |
| Ring | A tile of 25-story buildings, 1 to 40. One building holds the offices and halls of 7,771 people. | Yours (line 19) |
| Plant | A reactor site, water plant, sewage plant or waste works at the edge, 1 to 12 modules or units. One unit serves 60,000 people; one module makes 77 MW. *From ADR-0016, "at the edge" means on a circle's band.* | Mine (lines 20, 22) |
| Stretch | A length of mover line between two neighboring tiles, named `a\|b`. A tile the line touches has a stop under its center. | Mine |
| Need | What a layout lacks to keep its lines whole, typed: a ring or more ring buildings, reactor modules, plant units, schools near the neighborhoods whose pupils have no seat, playing fields, or the mover line run to the tiles it must reach. Each need names the checks it answers. Read off the assessment of an ordinary year, never worked out again, so the needs and the checks cannot disagree (ADR-0008). A check broken for want of people, or only on watch, asks for nothing to be built. | Mine |
| Proposal | A need with a tile chosen for it and the commands that would build it there, not yet built. The layout changes only when the player accepts it, through the ordinary commands (ADR-0008). | Mine |
| Command | What a player may do to the layout. It answers with an event or a rejection, never silently. | Ours |
| Line check | One line tested against the layout: idle (nothing to check yet), ok (holds), warn (watch), bad (broken). Each carries the tiles it marks and the facts behind its status. | Mine |
| Facts | The numbers behind a check's status, typed by check and case. The Board words them; the domain never writes a sentence. | Mine |
| Issue | Something wrong at one tile, typed. A tile's level is the worst of its issues. | Mine |
| Occupancy | The people living in each neighborhood this year, handed to the assessment (`assess`'s `occupied`, `assessCore`'s last parameter); a neighborhood left out holds its capacity, 5,000. Every check counts the people living there: a ring's load, pupils, trips, the daily list (short in any neighborhood below its hungriest need). The capital cost rows keep the homes as built; the yearly per-person rows follow the people (ADR-0009). | Yours (2026-10-14) |
| Day view | One day of a layout's people, hour by hour (`dayOf`, or `dayFrom` an assessment): trips, rides, mover trips, movers moving and parked, power, and pupils going to school in the hour before it starts and coming home in the hour it lets out. Built from the same assessment as the checks, so its trips sum to the day's (people times trips a person a day), its busiest hour is the ledger's peak, its most movers moving is the fleet less its reserve, and its highest power is the power check's peak. It holds no state; the month clock hands it the people (ADR-0009). The sized city's summer weekday: 147,727 trips, 14,773 between 5 and 6 p.m., 75 MW at 5 p.m., 45 MW at 5 a.m., 1,440 MWh, 188.5 of 216.7 movers moving at the busiest hour. | Mine |
| Stress | What is happening to the city in the year it is checked: more or fewer people, more children in every grade or in some grades, stretches closed. | Mine |
| Stretch test | The layout checked at an edge of the band, or with one stretch closed. A line *gives* when the stress leaves it worse off than in an ordinary year. | Mine |
| Bridge | A stretch with no way round it. The closure test closes bridges worst first, the one that cuts off the most neighborhoods ahead, until one breaks a line. | Mine |
| Seating | Pupils go to the nearest school with room; anyone left out is seated by a chain of moves, so nobody is counted out while a seat exists within the walk. | Mine |
| Score | What has been built, how trips are made, the fleet, the sub level, and the same city as a car city from the ledger. | Mine |
| Cost of what stands | Every tile and every stretch priced at the Ledger's premises in the six layers (ADR-0007 Decision 6), with the rows that belong to the city as a whole (the fleet and where it waits, upkeep, getting around), the Land and Financing lines, and the totals. A neighborhood is one center's share of the ledger's walking column, so a finished tile costs exactly its share; its walkway stands only where a stop does. A ring building is one building's worth of the middle's floor. A plant is its modules or units. A stretch is a step of mover floor and track. Schools and fields are inside the floor and the parks and add no row. The capital rows count the homes as built; the yearly per-person rows count the people living in them this year, stress included. The Ledger is the only home of a unit cost; the Site supplies the counts. | Mine |

## The sixteen line checks

| Check | Lines | Holds when |
|---|---|---|
| daily | 3, 13, 14 | A neighborhood's people feed every need on the daily list; the doctor's office, wanting 4,000, is the hungriest. |
| size | 3, 14 | The city has the 23,300 people urgent care wants and the 71,300 fine dining wants. Short is a watch, not a break. |
| ring | 10, 19 | Every ring has a building for each 7,771 people that use it. |
| work | 16 | Every neighborhood is within a 30-minute walk of its nearest ring; past it with a mover line is a watch, without one a break. |
| stops | 6 | Every neighborhood has a stop on a line that reaches its ring. |
| elementary, middle, high | 15, 16 | Every pupil has a seat within a 30-minute walk, no school over its cap. |
| fields | 4 | Every neighborhood is within a 20-minute walk of fields, or can ride to them. One nobody lives in yet only watches (ADR-0012 Decision 8, amended 2026-10-18). |
| ways | 17 | Under 0.5% of trips are past the walk with no mover to take. Trips toward a neighborhood nobody lives in yet are counted apart and only watched (ADR-0012 Decision 8, amended 2026-10-18). |
| power | 20, 21 | The modules carry the peak (1.5 kW a person) with one down for fuel. Broken two ways: `short`, they cannot carry it even with all running; `noSpare`, they carry it only while all run (ADR-0012 Decision 3 as amended 2026-10-18). |
| water, sewage, waste | 22 | Units serve the whole population. Sewage reports what it cleans against what the reactors take. |
| haul | 5 | Every plant is on the mover line, so nothing is hauled on the surface. |
| flow | 32 | *Decided 2026-10-20 (ADR-0016), the sixteenth check:* in every circle the sewage plant's outfall is downstream of the water plant's intake along the shore; idle while a circle lacks either plant; broken when an outfall is upstream of an intake, marking the plants it faults. A need a built thing mends: the missing plant on the right end of the band. |

Trips are counted over the real distances between tiles. Inside the walking limit people walk, with one in ten riding anyway if a mover runs. Past it, half of what is bikeable is biked and the rest rides if a mover runs, or is *stuck* if none does. A trip with nowhere to go (no seat, no fields, no ring) is counted apart, because the line it breaks is the one about that place.

## Scenarios

- **The ledger's city on the board** (`starterLayout`): 15 of 16 lines hold, fine dining to watch (14 of 15 before the `flow` check, ADR-0016). 71% of trips on foot, 11% by bike, 18% by mover; 217 movers against 42,000 cars; 1,399 acres against 5,857. The three band tests hold; a closure gives, because every stretch that serves a neighborhood is a spoke: ten spokes, and a run of six stretches south to the plants on the shore that cuts off no neighborhood.
- **Two neighborhoods at the circle's edge with no line** (`3,0`, `3,-1`): stops and ways break; 6.9% of trips are stuck; only the two are marked. Inside a circle every neighborhood is within the walk to work, so line 16 holds (ADR-0016; before the circles, two neighborhoods past the walk broke it too, with 9.1% stuck).
- **100,000 on spokes**: every line holds on 649 movers; a closure gives.
- **100,000 with loops**: every line holds, all four stretch tests hold, no bridges left, 584 movers, 36.3 km of line (30.4 before the plants moved to the shore, whose loops take eight stretches more).
- **The ledger's city needs nothing** (`needsOf`): no line a built thing can mend is broken. An empty site needs nothing either.
- **An eleventh neighborhood beside the ledger's city** (at `2,0`): it needs only the mover line run to it; the ring, plants and schools have room to spare. Past the walk to the ring (at `4,-2`) the same line also answers work.
- **Thirteen neighborhoods** (the ledger's city and `2,0`, `-3,0`, `0,2`): one more ring building, one more unit at each plant, an elementary and a high school for 180 and 120 pupils with no seat, fields near `0,2`, and the line to all three. Built, they leave no line broken and the tally at 15 of 16, fine dining to watch, which waits on people, not on anything built.
- **A lone neighborhood on an empty site** needs a whole city: a ring of 1 building, 2 reactor modules, a unit at each plant, a school of each kind, fields and a stop on a line to the ring.
- **Where each need goes** (`proposeFor`, `proposeNeighborhood` (where the next neighborhood goes: nearest the ring, else the neighborhoods, else beside the circle's middle, which is kept for the ring; ADR-0015 as narrowed by ADR-0016), `applyProposal`): one need at a time, its steps applied to a scratch layout and the needs read again, so what an addition calls for in turn is proposed after it. Ring buildings go on the ring that is short, or a new ring on its circle's middle when it is open, else the open tile in that circle nearest its neighborhoods; units go on a plant of the kind with room in the circle the need names, else a new plant on that circle's band's shore side, and the need has no room when that band is full (ADR-0016 Decision 5: water on the open shore tile farthest upstream, sewage farthest downstream of every water plant in its circle, a reactor or waste works nearest the sewage plant, the rest of the band when the shore is full; on a layout with no circles, the sketch's rule: open ground that borders no neighborhood or ring, nearest the line and farthest from the ring); a school or fields go on the neighborhood without one nearest those left short; the line runs by the fewest stretches from each tile it must reach to the line that joins the ring. Ties go to the tile first in the board's order. The eleventh neighborhood at `2,0` is proposed one stretch to `2,-1`, about 39 million dollars to build; thirteen are proposed one ring building, a unit at each plant, an elementary and a high school, fields at `0,2` and four stretches. Accepting every proposal leaves no line broken. A board with no open ground for a ring is told there is no room, and nothing is proposed past it.
- **The ledger's city sized to its people** (`sizedLayout`): the same tiles and line with 7 ring buildings and 2 reactor modules, one of each fewer; every line it held still holds and one fewer of either would break one. About 244,400 dollars a person to build against the starter's 267,600 and the car city's 261,800; 15,400 a year to run against 15,900 (243,900 and 267,100 before the starter's plants moved to the shore, two stretches more). `sizedTo` refuses a layout that still needs something.
- **A city still filling** (occupancy, ADR-0009): thirteen neighborhoods outgrow the ring and the plants, but while the three new ones stand empty the 50,000 living there leave the ring and every plant with room. A neighborhood of 400 still filling cannot yet feed its doctor's office, bakery, butcher or cafés: its daily list is a watch, not a break, since its shops open as people arrive (David, 2026-10-14), and only it is marked. A full neighborhood emptied by a stress below its hungriest need is still broken, and the Board names "the emptiest neighborhood". An empty neighborhood sends no pupils or trips and runs no per-person services, while its streets, parks and floor are still priced as built.
- **Who may move in** (`moveInGate`, ADR-0012): a neighborhood nobody lives in lacks its stop as a watch (line 6 amber, `waiting`), its fields the same way (line 4 `waiting`), trips toward it that would be stuck are a watch on line 17 (`waiting`), and with no school of a kind and no pupils the school check waits instead of breaking; once anyone lives there the missing stop, the far fields and the stuck trips are broken again. A neighborhood held to households with no school-age children sends no pupils and takes no seats. The gate reads the checks with the people the Run counts: it lacks water, sewage or waste exactly when that check is broken, power when the modules cannot carry its people's peak with all running (the `power` check `idle` or `short`, not `noSpare`: a city on its last module takes people in and shows lines 20 and 21 broken, ADR-0012 Decision 3 as amended 2026-10-18), and a school exactly when that check is broken at the neighborhood's tile. The ledger's city carries any of its neighborhoods; thirteen neighborhoods counted full leave the plants short for 65,000; a squeeze of 20% still fits the plants' 60,000 and 30% does not.
- **What the ledger's city on the board costs** (at the premises of 2026-10-13, `docs/brainstorm/future-city/money.md`): about 14.0 billion dollars to build (groundworks 4.5, buildings 9.4, fleet 0.03) and 592 million a year to run, against the ledger column's 10.5 billion and 520 million: the board stands 3 reactor modules and 3 plant units the column does not count, and the band's 8 ring buildings where the column prices 6.4 buildings' floor. An empty site costs nothing.

- **The circles and the band** (ADR-0016; `docs/work/archive/concentric-site/plan.md`, scenarios of Phases 2 to 5):
  - Given the empty site, then it holds one circle at `0,0`, whose hex rings hold 6, 12, 18 and 24 tiles, the fourth the band, and whose band's south tiles touch the shore; every tile of the rectangle is in a circle or open to nothing.
  - Given a plant unit added on a home ring, then it is refused `OutsideBand` and the layout is unchanged; given a neighborhood or a ring building on a band tile, `InsideBand`; given any placing command, a stretch included, on a tile outside every circle, `OutsideCircle`; given `erase` there, `NothingThere`.
  - Given a stored layout with a plant in ring 3, when it is read back, then the plant is dropped as an off-board tile is; given one with no circles, then it restores with the first.
  - Given the starter layout, then its four plants stand on the shore side of the first circle's band, each on the line, the water plant upstream of the sewage plant, and the tally is still 14 of 15 lines held (fine dining to watch) with `flow` holding, so 15 of 16 when the Board counts the sixteenth.
  - Given a circle with a water plant and a sewage plant, when the sewage plant is upstream of the water plant in the current's direction, then `flow` is broken and marks both; given a circle lacking either, then it is idle; given the starter, it holds. Given `flow` broken, then `needsOf` asks for the missing plant on the right end of the band and `proposeFor` places it there.
  - Given one neighborhood on an empty circle, when every need is proposed and accepted in turn, then the ring stands on the circle's middle, every plant is on the band's shore side, every neighborhood inside the band, and no line is broken; given a vetoed piece (`proposeFor`'s avoid list, the run's vetoes), then it is proposed elsewhere when it can be and has no room when every place for it is vetoed; given 36 neighborhoods placed by `proposeNeighborhood` one after another, then they fill hex rings 1 to 3 nearest the ring first and the 37th call answers null, the circle being full; given 20 neighborhoods and 2 units of each plant and 3 modules, then every line holds at 100,000 people.
  - Given `openCircle` at `9,0` on the starter, then `CircleOpened` and the layout holds two circles whose bands touch at a seam; given it at `4,0`, `CircleOverlaps`; given it at `20,0`, `OffBoard`. Given two circles, then a neighborhood in the second is within the walk of the second ring, not the first, and is served by its own circle: the ring and the four plant checks are judged circle by circle (a layout with no circles is one), a circle whose neighborhoods have no ring reads `none` naming it, a short circle is named in the check's `short` list, each `ring`, `modules` and `plantUnits` need names its circle and is met in that circle's band or has no room there, the move-in gate holds a neighborhood only while its own circle is short, and `proposeNeighborhood` fills the first circle before the next; the line joins a neighborhood to its own ring and then its network to the first ring's, crossing the seam from band to band, so that `haul` holds for both circles' plants and every trip has a mover.

## Acceptance

`circle.test.ts` asserts the first scenario of "The circles and the band": one circle at `0,0` on the empty site and the starter; rings of 6, 12, 18 and 24 and 61 tiles, all on the board; the band's five south tiles on the shore for each circle; the 158 tiles of the board in the first circle (61), the second (61) or neither (36); that two circles 8 steps apart share `4,0` and 9 steps apart touch; that a circle fits only when its whole band is on the board; that a restored layout keeps the circles that fit, once each, and falls back to the first; and that every command's event path, an accepted proposal and a restore on two circles answer with the circles they were given. `fill.test.ts` asserts the sixth scenario: one neighborhood on an empty circle proposed every need with room, the ring at the middle, the plants on the shore, no line broken; 36 neighborhoods by `proposeNeighborhood` filling rings 1 to 3 with the 37th call null, and 20 of them holding every line at 100,000 on 2 units of each plant and 3 modules; and `proposeFor`'s avoid list: a vetoed water tile sends the plant to the next shore tile upstream of the sewage plant, every band tile vetoed leaves the need with no room, a vetoed stretch is routed round, a vetoed school goes to another neighborhood. `band.test.ts` asserts the band rule: `OutsideBand`, `InsideBand` and `OutsideCircle` from every placing command with the layout unchanged, `NothingThere` from `erase` outside the circles, the line crossing a seam on two circles and a circle-less layout refusing none of it; what a restore drops; the starter's plants on the shore, on the line, water upstream of sewage, `flow` holding and 15 of 16; the `flow` check idle, reversed with both plants marked and mended by a water plant upstream, judged circle by circle and counting only the circles with both plants; the intake need and its proposal; and where a new plant goes: on a lone neighborhood every plant on the shore with the water farthest upstream, the reactor beside the sewage plant and the flow holding once built, the band when the shore is full, the next circle when the band is full, and the farthest downstream tile for sewage when none lies below the water. `second.test.ts` asserts the seventh scenario: `openCircle` at `9,0` on the starter answers `CircleOpened` with the bands touching at a seam and no tile in both, at `4,0`, `5,0`, `0,0` or on a standing middle `CircleOverlaps` naming the shared tile, at `20,0` or `12,0` `OffBoard`, the layout unchanged; a neighborhood at `8,0` has no ring until one stands at `9,0`, the first ring's load untouched, then walks to `9,0`; its circle's water, sewage, waste and power are short while only the first circle's plants stand, the gate holds it and not a first-circle neighborhood, and four plants on its shore serve it with `flow` ordered in two circles; the next neighborhood goes in the first circle until it holds 36 and then beside the second middle at `9,-1`; a full first circle and the second circle's first neighborhood, proposed every need, have the second ring at `9,0`, its four plants in its band, at least one stretch crossing the seam band to band, `haul` reached, `work` within, `flow` ordered, no line broken and nothing needed; and a layout with no circles is judged as one. `outline.test.ts` pins the shore board: rows -4 to 4, east-west positions -4 to 13, 18 tiles on even rows and 17 on odd, its corners, its edge neighbors, and that it keeps the sketch's tiles within its rows in the sketch's order.


`domain/site/test/parity.test.ts` matches 15 recorded assessments (checks, every tile's report, the score, the stretch tests), 29 moves, 2 seatings, 4 restores and 4 bridge cases against the sketch's `engine.js`. `scenarios.test.ts` asserts the scenarios above in the language of the lines. `cost.test.ts` asserts that an empty site costs nothing, that a finished neighborhood costs the ledger's per-tile share row by row, that plants and stretches are priced at the groundworks premises, that the layers sum to the whole, that a squeeze moves the yearly rows and not the buildings, that a changed premise moves only its rows, and, loosely, that the board's city lands near the column's. `occupancy.test.ts` asserts the city-still-filling scenario, that leaving occupancy out is the same assessment figure for figure, and the refusals. `sized.test.ts` asserts the sized city's scenario, that the starter is untouched, and that sizing a sized city changes nothing; `cost.test.ts` pins its cost against the starter's and the car city's. `propose.test.ts` asserts that on five layouts every proposal is on the board, on open ground or a tile of its own kind, and that accepting all of them through the ordinary commands leaves no line broken; that new plants keep off the ground beside the homes; that a full board says there is no room; and that `applyProposal` answers with every step's event or the first rejection and leaves its layout as it was. `movein.test.ts` asserts the who-may-move-in scenario, and that the gate lacks a service or a school exactly when that check is broken for the neighborhood on six occupancies. `needs.test.ts` asserts the needs scenarios above, that the needs answer exactly the broken checks a built thing can mend on every layout it is given, and that `needsOf` leaves its layout as it was. The parity fixture is unchanged: money adds figures to the score (`vehicleTrips`, `rideKm`) and the record compares only the recorded keys. The sketch's sentences are kept in the fixture for the Board's wording tests in Phase 4. `movein.test.ts` asserts the gate and the checks it reads (ADR-0012 Decisions 3, 8 and 9): a neighborhood nobody lives in watches its stop, its far fields, the stuck trips toward it and its schools, and each breaks again with one resident; and the power check's three readings on a lone neighborhood: no module is short, one module carries its 7.5 MW peak only while it runs (`noSpare`, broken, the gate lacks nothing of power), two carry it with one down. `needs.test.ts` asserts that a `noSpare` site still asks for the spare module.
