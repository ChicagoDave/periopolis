# ADR-0007: Cost is a derived consequence of the ledger, priced from placeholders

**Date**: 2026-10-13
**Kind**: technical
**Scope**: domain/ledger
**Status**: ACCEPTED

## Context

The brainstorm's "Next" item 3 is the one word "Money", and its open list says "what any of it costs" is still unknown (`docs/brainstorm/future-city/overview.md`). ADR-0003 Decision 4 names money as its own plan after the port. The ledger already counts nearly everything a city is made of, per city and per person, for both columns, by one arithmetic the car column vouches for. The question was where money enters and how far it reaches: a price on every count, or a budget the clock plays with, or a business model for the tool itself. The plan (`docs/work/archive/money/plan.md`) reads it as the first, to be confirmed by the figures it prints.

## Decision

1. Cost is a tenth step of the ledger: a **consequence**, never set, derived once per column from the counts the nine steps give and a list of **unit costs**. `deriveCarCity` and `deriveWalkingCity` take the unit costs as a last parameter and return `cost` beside every other figure.
2. Every unit cost is a **premise with a mark**: a placeholder in `COSTS` that David can change, and never a figure hard-coded in the derivation. *Amended 2026-10-13: the mark is Yours, Sourced or Mine (Decision 9); Phase 1's premises were all Mine.* A cost row is exactly one premise times one ledger count, with both kept on the row, so a reader can trace every dollar to the count it prices and the placeholder it was priced at.
3. **Both columns are priced by the same function.** A column lists what it has (the car city has parking and cars; yours has the sub level, the stops, the pipe, the dumbwaiters, the lanes and the movers), and one arithmetic prices the lists and totals them. A difference between the columns is a difference in what they build and run, never in how the money is added up.
4. Two kinds of money: **capital** to build, and **running** cost for a year. Each is a total and a per-person figure. Running cost holds an upkeep row (a share of what was built, vehicles aside), vehicle-kilometers, vehicles worn out, goods runs, and porters where there are any. *Amended 2026-10-13 after the money brainstorm (`docs/brainstorm/future-city/money.md`): running cost also holds a civic layer and a households layer; see Decisions 8 to 11.*
5. **Floor is priced once.** Buildings are priced by the square meter of floor the ledger already fixes: low-rise floor (homes, shops, schools, clinics) and the ring's high-rise floor. Needs and schools are inside that floor and are not priced again by count; the plan's "needs by count" was dropped for that reason.
6. **Plants are not ledger counts.** Reactor modules and plant units are Site concepts (`docs/domain/site.md`, Mine, lines 20 and 22), so their unit costs enter here as premises and the Site prices the modules and units that stand. The same holds for everything placed on a layout: the Ledger is the only place a unit cost lives, and the Site and the Run derive the cost of what stands and what is ordered from the Ledger's premises times their own counts. ADR-0005 Decision 2 split Site from Ground by the question each answers; "what does what stands cost" is a Site question because the Site owns what stands.
7. **Money adds rows and moves nothing.** Every figure of the nine steps is identical with and without a cost premise, the parity suite compares the old rows only, and the published figures do not move. The wording of money (dollars, millions, "per person") is the Board's (ADR-0004); the Ledger emits numbers and row ids.

8. **Six layers** (line 25). Cost is reported in six layers, each a set of rows with its own total, the whole the sum of the six: to build, **groundworks** (streets and paths, the sub level, the pipe, the plants, the ground where vehicles wait, parks, lanes), **buildings** (floor by the square meter, dumbwaiters) and **fleet** (cars or movers); to run each year, **upkeep** (a share of what was built, structures only), **civic services** (schools per pupil; police, fire, health and library per person; electricity, water, sewage and waste per unit, with the per-person counts of lines 20 to 22 entering the ledger as premises marked Mine) and **households** (getting around: vehicle-kilometers and vehicles worn out). Land and financing are their own lines outside the six. *Amended 2026-10-14 (`docs/work/archive/widen-civic-layer/plan.md`, Phase 2): the city carries the state's share (line 27), so the civic layer also holds public welfare, higher education, financial administration, corrections and courts per person, and the running of the streets and the parks per m² of each column's own street and park. Housing and community development, air transportation, natural resources and the unclassified remainder are left out, with the reasons in `money.md` ("Civic categories not yet counted"); interest on debt is the Financing line.*
9. **Every unit cost carries a mark** (line 26): Yours, Sourced (with the source and its year) or Mine. The mark is part of the premise, the Board shows it, and a figure stays a placeholder whatever its mark until David accepts it. The sources found on 2026-10-13 are tabled in `docs/brainstorm/future-city/money.md`. *Amended 2026-10-14 (`docs/work/archive/accept-money-premises/plan.md`, Phase 2): a Sourced mark carries whether David has accepted it. An accepted Sourced figure is his and keeps its source, year and link; a Mine figure he accepts becomes Yours, so only a Sourced mark carries the flag. The Board shows "Sourced 2024, accepted" and words it "Accepted." where an unaccepted one says "A placeholder until accepted."*
10. **Housing is paid for once** (line 28). The buildings layer prices every square meter; the Financing line spreads capital over a term at a rate (a 30-year municipal bond as the placeholder); no rent or mortgage row exists. The Land line prices the city's area.
11. **Cost to the city as a whole** (line 27). One figure per row. The ledger does not say who pays; a split between taxes, bonds, rent, fares and fees is a later decision with its own premises.
12. **The sub level is built floor, not tunnel.** David: "there could be multiple sub-levels, constructed from bottom up." The walkway is priced as floor by the square meter; the mover line as a floor plus its track and systems; the pipe as a duct in that floor. Transit tunnelling costs do not apply to it.
13. **The car column is vouched** against what America pays: the stock of structures per person, schools per pupil, AAA's cost of owning a car, and local direct spending per person. The vouching checks are tests in the ledger, loose by design.

## Consequences

- `domain/ledger/src/cost.ts` holds the premises, the row ids, the pricing and the two column lists. A new thing the city builds is a new row id, a new count from the ledger, and a new unit cost in `COSTS`, in one change; the type checker reports a row the Board has not worded.
- The Site and the Run, in `docs/work/archive/money/plan.md` Phase 3, price what stands from `COSTS` and their own counts. They do not carry unit costs of their own.
- *Added 2026-10-13, Phase 3:* the pricing arithmetic is the Ledger's too. `priceCounts` and `tallyCost` are exported, and the Site's `costOf` (`domain/site/src/cost.ts`) and the Run's `spendOf` (`domain/run/src/spend.ts`) go through them. A neighborhood is one center's share of the walking column, so a finished tile costs exactly its share; plants are modules and units at the groundworks premises, in two row ids (`reactorModules`, `plantUnits`) that no ledger column carries. The Run's record row carries `standing` and `spend` by layer: a built layer's spend in a year is what stands at the end of it less what stood a year before, so a take-down or a shrinking fleet comes off; a yearly layer's spend is the year's bill; year 0 spends nothing. The sketch's clock has no money, so the Run's parity compares the record's old fields only, as Decision 7 has the ledger's parity do. A stored row without money restores zeros.
- The premises are dollars of their source's year and are not tuned. *Amended 2026-10-13:* at the premises tabled in `money.md`, the published city costs about 255 thousand dollars a person to build in the car column and 210 in yours, about 14,900 against 10,400 a year to run, and about 12,900 against 12,600 a year to finance the structures. Those figures are pinned in `cost.test.ts` as a check on the premises, not as truth. (At Phase 1's all-Mine placeholders they were 248 against 247 and 5,900 against 3,200.) *Amended 2026-10-14:* with every premise accepted (`docs/work/archive/accept-money-premises/plan.md`), about 262 thousand against 198 to build, 15,400 against 10,000 a year to run, and 13,300 against 11,900 a year to finance; the board's opening city costs 13.4 billion to build and 571 million a year to run. The car column vouches inside the band for structures and schools, at 42% of AAA for a car's year (short drives, a 17-year life), and under the band for civic less schools (services the ledger does not count, in both columns alike; `money.md`, "Vouching"). *Amended 2026-10-14 (`docs/work/archive/widen-civic-layer/plan.md`):* with the civic layer widened (Decision 8 as amended), about 20,200 against 14,500 a year to run; building and financing do not move; the board's opening city costs 13.4 billion to build and 794 million a year to run. The civic band was restated to match its source: the car column's government services, 7,069 a person, are 81% of state and local spending less schools (8,760), and the rest is the categories left out and interest in Financing; the whole civic layer is 105% of 11,087.
- Changing a placeholder moves exactly the rows that use it, and the test suite asserts that. A budget the clock spends, or who pays, is a new decision, not a change to a placeholder.
- *Added 2026-10-13:* `cost.ts` is restructured by layer: a row carries its layer and its premise's mark; `CostFigures` totals each layer, the two kinds, and the Land and Financing lines. The Phase 1 pins on the published city move with the sourced premises and are re-pinned.
- *Added 2026-10-13:* electricity, water, sewage and waste per person enter the ledger as premises so the civic layer can price them; their citations are owed (`overview.md`, lines 20 to 23) and the figures are marked Mine until then. *Amended 2026-10-14:* cited and accepted (`money.md`, "Accepted"). Water is no longer a fixed figure per column: `waterGpd` derives it from a base and a watering depth on the column's yards and parks, the same arithmetic for both columns and the Site (Decision 3), so the car city's 138 and your city's 116 gallons a person a day are consequences of their green ground.
- *Added 2026-10-13:* the Board words the marks and the layers (ADR-0004); a row without a wording or a premise without a mark fails the type check.
