# ADR-0016: The site is concentric circles — an outer band for the plants, and a second circle for growth past the first

**Date**: 2026-10-20
**Kind**: technical
**Scope**: domain/site, domain/run, apps/board
**Status**: ACCEPTED

## Context

David, 2026-10-20: "We should alter the map. It should be concentric circles and an outermost band is where infrastructure resides. And that band should be far enough out to allow for 100,000 residents and then a second main circle would be needed for further population growth."

Today the site is a rectangle of 115 hexagonal tiles (ADR-0006 Decision 3), and a plant goes wherever the player puts it or, when the run orders it (ADR-0015), on an open tile that borders no neighborhood or ring, nearest the line and farthest from the ring. Nothing keeps a later neighborhood off the tile beside a plant, so a plant at today's edge is interior tomorrow. The starter layout, the sketch's city of 50,000, has its ring at `0,0`, its neighborhoods in hex rings 1 and 2, and its four plants in hex ring 3.

*Added 2026-10-20, during the interview:* David asked for first principles, "what are the local natural resources? waterways, runoff, great lakes, rivers... these all enable certain levels of population", and chose a lake shore on one of the Great Lakes (`docs/brainstorm/future-city/setting.md`, lines 30 to 32). Rain on the city alone yields about 50 gallons a person a day (arithmetic in `setting.md`, Mine: 93.3 acres × 36 inches × 27,154 gallons an acre-inch, over 365 days and 5,000 people), under half the 116 drawn, so the city draws from the lake; on a Great Lake supply never caps the circle, and the rule is the return flow and the order of intake and outfall along the shore.

The lines fix the geometry. A 30-minute walk to work or school (line 16) is 2 km, 3.03 tile steps (read 2026-10-20 from the shipped rates: `RATES.longKm` 2 over `RATES.stepKm` 0.660), so the neighborhoods within a walk of a central ring fill hex rings 1 to 3 around it: 6, 12 and 18 tiles, 36 neighborhoods, 180,000 people at 5,000 a tile. 100,000 residents is 20 neighborhoods, rings 1 and 2 and two tiles of ring 3. A band at ring 3 would leave room for only 90,000, so the band David asks for is hex ring 4: 24 tiles, beyond the walk, holding the reactor site and the water, sewage and waste plants (line 22). One ring of up to 40 buildings carries 310,000, so one ring serves a circle; 100,000 people need 2 units of each plant and 3 reactor modules. Line 5 keeps every plant on the mover line, so distance from the middle costs a stretch of line, not hauling; line 11 graduates height from the outer edges to the middle, which a works between the ring and the homes would break; line 22 has reclaimed water cool the reactors, which puts the sewage plant beside the reactor site.

## Decision

1. **The site is a circle, and a circle is a central ring with three rings of homes and a band.** The circle's middle tile holds its ring; hex rings 1 to 3 around it hold neighborhoods, every one within the 30-minute walk of the ring; hex ring 4 is the **band**, where the plants stand. The circle, the band and a tile's ring distance from the circle's middle are values of the Site.
   - **The outline stays a rectangle, laid along the shore** (David, 2026-10-20). ADR-0006's `rectangle(halfWidth, halfHeight)` is kept as the site's shape; only its two figures change, to hold two circles side by side along the shore with a margin. The lake is one long edge of the rectangle, off the board. Circles are values laid out on the board, each band touching the shore; ground outside every circle is off limits until a circle claims it, so the second circle's site is visible from the first day and the Board's frame never changes.
   - **The shore is the south edge.** The first circle's middle is `0,0`, so every key the starter uses today stays where it is, and its band's southmost tiles (row `r = 4`) touch the shore; the second circle's middle is `8,0`. The rectangle runs from row -4 to row 4 and from 4 steps west of the first middle to 4 steps east of the second, with no margin beyond the bands: two circles of 61 tiles and the ground between their rings' reach. ADR-0006 Decision 2 centres a rectangle on `0,0`; it is amended by pointer so a rectangle is an extent (west, east, north, south in tile steps and rows), of which the centred form is the special case; the tile order of Decision 4 is unchanged.
   - **A layout carries its circles.** `Layout.circles` lists each circle's middle; `emptyLayout` and the starter hold the first. `openCircle(layout, middle)` lays out another: it answers `CircleOpened`, or `OffBoard` when its band would leave the board, or `CircleOverlaps` when a tile of it is already another circle's. A circle is ground, not a piece: no crew, no years, no price; it is open the moment the command answers.
2. **Plants stand in the band, and nothing else does.** A reactor site, water plant, sewage plant or waste works goes on a band tile; a neighborhood or a ring goes inside the band. **This is a hard rule of the Site** (David, 2026-10-20): `addPlantUnit` off a band refuses with `OutsideBand`, `placeNeighborhood` and `addRingBuilding` on a band tile refuse with `InsideBand`, and every placing command (`placeNeighborhood`, `addRingBuilding`, `addPlantUnit`, `layStretch`, `toggleExtra`) on a tile outside every circle refuses with `OutsideCircle`, so the line runs only through circles and crosses the seam where two bands touch; `erase` on such a tile answers `NothingThere` as today; each is a typed rejection the Board words (ADR-0004), and the layout is unchanged. `restoreLayout` drops what the band forbids, as it drops what the outline does not admit, and a stored layout with no circles restores with the first; there are no saved cities from before this decision (David, 2026-10-20), so nothing is migrated. Proposals go through the same commands, so `proposeFor` and `proposeNeighborhood` never name a tile the band refuses. Every layout on the circle board is band-legal by construction.
3. **A second circle carries growth past the first.** When the first circle is full, a second circle with its own ring, rings of homes and band is laid out beside it and the mover line joins them. **Its middle is 8 steps from the first along the shore** (David, 2026-10-20), so the two bands touch at a seam and each circle is whole, with its own band, intake and outfall ordered along the current (line 32); no tile belongs to two circles. **The Run opens it** on a run with `infer` (ADR-0015): the month `proposeNeighborhood` finds no room in the first circle, the run orders the second circle through `placeOrder` with `openCircle` as the layout command, as it orders anything, a typed fact (`CircleOpened`) on the month's row says so, and the player's veto applies to it as to any inferred order. A run without `infer` never opens one; the player has no command for it in this decision. **A circle is full when every home tile within the walk is taken** (David, 2026-10-20): 36 neighborhoods, 180,000 people, the walk's figure. The run opens the second circle the month `proposeNeighborhood` finds no open tile inside the first circle's band, never earlier; the 100,000 of David's words is what the band is sized to hold with room, not the trigger. At 4,800 arrivals a year (ADR-0009) a circle fills in about forty years from an empty site, so the real-path run that reaches the second circle runs longer than ADR-0015's twenty.
4. **The parity oracle stays the sketch's hexagon, and the starter moves to the band** (ADR-0003, ADR-0006 Decision 5; David, 2026-10-20). The parity suite replays the sketch's layouts on `hexagon(5)` from its own fixtures, where the plants sit in ring 3 and no circle is laid out, so the band rule never reaches them. The shipped starter layout, the city the Board opens on, is band-legal: its four plants move from ring 3 to the shore side of the first circle's band, each reached by the line, intake upstream of outfall; so do the 100,000-person layouts the scenarios hold. The 14-of-15 tally stays; the cost figures move by the added stretches and are re-pinned where the browser flows and ADR-0007's records state them. The starter is no longer the sketch's tile for tile, and ADR-0008's "the starter stays the sketch's" is amended by a note pointing here.
5. **The band faces the lake** (lines 30 to 32). The shore is one edge of the site and the water is off the board. The water plant's intake, the sewage plant, the reactors its reclaimed water cools, and the outfall stand on the shore side of the band, intake upstream of outfall along the current; a second circle goes along the shore, never between the first circle's intake and its outfall. Supply is not the circle's ceiling (line 31 on a Great Lake); the walk (line 16) and the plants (line 22) stay the binding lines. **One new check, for line 32** (David, 2026-10-20): `flow` holds when every circle's sewage outfall stands downstream of its water intake along the shore, idle while a circle lacks either plant, broken when an outfall is upstream of an intake, marking the plants it faults; along the shore means by a tile's east-west position, `q + r/2` steps east of `0,0`, and the current's direction is a signed premise of the site (`SiteRates.current`, +1 eastward or -1 westward, Mine until David names the lake and the shore), so the check compares positions of a circle's water and sewage tiles in the current's direction. Lines 5 and 11 gain no check: the hard rule of Decision 2 holds a plant on the band and a home off it by construction, so nothing is left for a check to find; line 5's haul check and line 11's tile stay as they are. The sixteenth check joins `NEED_CHECKS` so a broken `flow` is a need a built thing mends, which a proposal answers by placing the missing plant on the right end of the band.
6. **Amends by pointer.** ADR-0006 Decision 2 (a rectangle is centred) is amended to an extent. ADR-0008 Consequences ("the starter stays the sketch's") is amended for the plants' tiles. ADR-0006 Decision 3's two figures change to hold two circles along the shore; its shape, and Decisions 1, 2, 4 and 5, stand. ADR-0015 Decision 3 (`proposeNeighborhood` picks the open tile nearest the ring) is narrowed to the tiles inside the band. The session that flips this ADR to ACCEPTED writes those notes in the same commit.

## Consequences

- `openCircle` is a new command with its event and two rejections, worded by the Board; `Layout.circles` is new stored state, restored with the first circle when absent.
- The Site gains the circle, the band and the ring distance as values on the rectangle; `allTiles`, `onBoard`, `neighbors` and `adjacent` are unchanged, and `SiteRates.outline` is a wider rectangle. A tile outside every circle is on the board and open to nothing until a circle claims it.
- `flow` is a sixteenth line check with its facts and Board wording, and the tally the Board shows becomes "of 16"; the parity fixtures, which record the fifteen, compare the recorded keys only (ADR-0003 Decision 4 as amended by ADR-0007).
- `OutsideBand`, `InsideBand` and `OutsideCircle` are new rejection shapes with their Board wording in the same change (ADR-0004); the Board's needs card and the run's inference never meet them, since the proposals keep to the band.
- The run's inference (ADR-0015) reads the band through `proposeFor` and `proposeNeighborhood`; the second circle is a third signal, read when the homes signal finds no room, and `CircleOpened` is a new fact shape with its Board wording in the same change.
- The second circle's plants come before its homes, as the crews' classes already order (ADR-0012 Decision 2); its first neighborhood is admitted when its own band serves it.
- The browser flows pinned on 115 drawn tiles, and every figure pinned on the starter's cost, are re-pinned when the Board draws the circle board and the starter's plants stand in the band.
- The founding record in ADR-0015 (48,379, 56,349 and 53,698 people in twenty years) is the baseline the real-path run of this plan compares against at twenty years; the run to the second circle is a longer run, about forty years, recorded here when it is made.
- A different trigger (a premise figure, a player control) is a change to this decision, not to the signals.
